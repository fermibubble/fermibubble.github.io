Principles

Nine commitments for systems that deserve production authority. Each principle connects a philosophical question to a concrete engineering practice.

Nine design commitments

The principles

01

Verdicts require Epistemics

Every conclusion carries its justification, uncertainty, alternatives, and conditions for reassessment.

5 min read ↗ 02

Evidence requires Provenance

Every material claim keeps a trail back to its source, transformations, and limitations.

4 min read ↗ 03

State requires Ownership

Every persistent fact has an authoritative owner, a version, and rules for how it can change.

4 min read ↗ 04

Autonomy requires a Dial

Authority is explicit, specific to each action, proportionate to risk, and revocable.

4 min read ↗ 05

Inputs require a Trust Boundary

Evidence remains data. Permissions and policy arrive through separate, authenticated control paths.

4 min read ↗ 06

Knowledge requires a Clock

Every decision-relevant fact records when it was true, when it was learned, and when it must be reconsidered.

4 min read ↗ 07

Delegation requires Ceilings

Delegated authority narrows, and shared limits bound the cost and impact of the entire agent tree.

4 min read ↗ 08

Failure requires a Ladder

Partial failures lead to explicit degraded modes that preserve essential safety and expose missing evidence.

4 min read ↗ 09

Learning requires Outcomes

Independent outcomes grade decisions over time, and evaluated lessons improve future reviews.

4 min read ↗

Read these as design commitments. Their value is in what the system still guarantees when evidence is missing, an agent is mistaken, or a human changes their mind.
