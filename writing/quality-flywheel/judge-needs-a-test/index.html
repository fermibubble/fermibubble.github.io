The Judge Also Needs a Test

An evaluator is another system with blind spots.

Suppose we show a grader two Atlas answers. The first correctly identifies the expired certificate. The second preserves the confident tone and citations but changes the cause to excessive CPU load. Does the verdict change?

If it does not, we have learned something more useful than the grader's average agreement on ordinary examples. We have found a controlled counterexample to the behavior we expected it to measure.

The 2026 paper Judging LLM-as-a-Judge probes this issue directly. On HealthBench and ResearchRubrics, the authors find that properties of the rubric alone can predict some judgments and that some counterfactual changes fail to produce the expected judgment change. This does not show that judges ignore every answer. It shows why normal-case agreement should be supplemented by adversarial and counterfactual checks. [7]

Different questions also deserve different instruments. A parser can establish that a URL is present. It cannot establish that the source supports the diagnosis. A functional test can establish that a repaired system satisfies its assertions. It cannot establish every untested property of the repair.

Choose the instrument for the claim
ClaimStarting instrumentIndependent check
Required structure is presentParser or schemaBoundary and malformed cases
The action achieved its goalState comparison or executable testTest adequacy; unintended changes
The explanation matches the evidenceCalibrated semantic judgeCounterexamples and expert labels
The answer is useful in this domainDomain rubric and expert reviewReal outcomes and disagreement review

Cheap evaluation can still be sophisticated. RocketEval combines instance-specific checklists, a small judge, and supervised reweighting. On its MT-Bench and WildBench evaluation, it reports strong correlation with human preferences and more than a fiftyfold cost reduction. That establishes a promising design for those evaluations; it does not establish that a small judge can safely replace experts on every agent trajectory. [8]

Keep expert labels as an anchor. Prediction-Powered Inference offers a statistical way to combine plentiful predictions with a smaller labeled sample, correcting estimated population quantities using observed prediction errors. Its guarantees rely on the sampling and inference setup; repeatedly sending only suspicious cases to humans is not the same as a representative audit. [9]

Check false alarms, missed failures, repeatability, and whether the grader preserves the release decisions you care about. Inspect these by criterion family. An apparently excellent overall agreement rate may simply reflect that almost every example passes.

THE POWERFUL IDEA

The evaluation system needs its own improvement loop—and a frozen version during each comparison.

For Atlas, this means keeping paired examples that differ in diagnosis while retaining the same style. A judge that cannot distinguish them should not decide whether the diagnostic fix ships.

    First posted: 2026-08-31

    Counterfactual and rubric-only probes of judges. Accepted for EMNLP 2026; findings concern the evaluated benchmarks and do not establish a universal causal mechanism.

    First posted: 2025-03-07

    Statistical inference using predictions plus labeled data. Its sampling assumptions matter; targeted escalation alone is not a representative audit.
