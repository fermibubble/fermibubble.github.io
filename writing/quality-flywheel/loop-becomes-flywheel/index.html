When a Loop Becomes a Flywheel

Repetition compounds only when the system retains something useful.

Run the agent. Read the failures. Change the prompt. Run it again. This is a loop. It might produce progress, but it can also consume a month while rediscovering the same problems.

A flywheel adds memory. One failure becomes a reproducible case. Several cases reveal a shared mechanism. The mechanism suggests a reusable tool or procedure. A verified fix becomes a regression test. The next investigation starts with better instruments and less uncertainty.

The central assets in the diagram matter as much as the arrows. A log archive by itself is not accumulated understanding. A hundred nearly identical tests may add maintenance work without adding much information. Useful accumulation means cases that distinguish alternatives, standards that reflect user needs, tools that remove recurring obstacles, and evidence that records what actually helped.

Two kinds of feedback coexist here. Corrective feedback detects a deviation and helps reduce it: Atlas misdiagnoses the incident, so we investigate and repair that behavior. Reinforcing feedback improves the capacity to learn: the resulting case and verifier make future diagnosis changes easier to assess. The word “flywheel” is a useful metaphor for this second effect.

THE POWERFUL IDEA

The compounding asset is reusable knowledge. Benchmark scores need not grow exponentially.

Consider two teams that both fix ten bugs. The first leaves behind ten edits and ten closed tickets. The second also leaves behind a small library of failure cases, a clearer taxonomy, and verified checks. On bug eleven, they no longer have the same starting position.

This is an architectural argument, rather than a claim that every extra test increases quality. A flywheel can accumulate friction: stale references, biased graders, duplicated cases, and rules that conflict. Maintenance includes retiring weak checks and revalidating old lessons.

For Atlas, the first durable asset is a recorded incident where the evidence establishes an expired certificate. The next is a criterion that checks whether its diagnosis follows from that evidence. We now have a way to test proposed improvements without relying on a persuasive demo.
