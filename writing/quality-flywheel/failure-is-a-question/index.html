A Failure Is a Question

The final mistake tells you where the damage appeared. The trace may reveal where it began.

Atlas recommends a restart. “Bad reasoning” is an easy label. It is also a poor engineering plan.

Perhaps the certificate check was unavailable. Perhaps the tool returned the expiry date but the assistant ignored it. Perhaps retrieval supplied a runbook for the wrong failure. Perhaps the benchmark's expected answer described a different incident. These explanations produce the same disappointing answer and require different repairs.

EARLY ASSUMPTION“Probably overload”
↓
EVIDENCE SELECTIONReads CPU metrics; skips certificate status
↓
VISIBLE SYMPTOMRecommends an ineffective restart

A scalar score compresses this sequence into failure. The trajectory preserves possible explanations. Yet reading a trace is not the same as proving a cause.

Who&When makes the difficulty concrete: in its multi-agent failure-attribution experiments, the best method identifies the responsible agent with 53.5% accuracy but the decisive step with only 14.2%. These figures describe the studied systems and methods, rather than a ceiling for newer ones. [10]

More recent work adds structure. AgentRx derives constraints, checks execution steps, and assembles evidence for diagnosis. DeFA, posted on 1 October 2026, represents dependencies and error propagation so that an early source of failure can be distinguished from its later symptoms. Its downstream skill-improvement results concern spreadsheet tasks; wider transfer remains to be demonstrated. [11] [12]

These approaches suggest a better next question: what intervention would distinguish our explanations? Give Atlas the certificate status directly while leaving the rest of the task comparable. If it now diagnoses correctly, missing access or retrieval becomes a stronger candidate. If it still recommends a restart, the problem may lie in evidence use or decision policy. Inspect whether the intervention changed anything else.

Run the intervention across several representative failures and appropriate controls. A candidate that fixes certificate incidents but makes every incident look like a certificate incident has learned another shortcut.

THE POWERFUL IDEA

A diagnosis earns credibility when its proposed intervention produces the predicted change.

Useful failure records therefore contain more than a label. Save the observations, competing explanations, discriminating intervention, result, and remaining uncertainty. This gives the optimizer something specific to test and gives reviewers a way to disagree productively.
