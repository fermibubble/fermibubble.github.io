The Mountain Is Made of Metrics

Change the scoring rule and you change which direction counts as uphill.

We often discuss the metric as if it were a thermometer attached to the product. During optimization, it also acts as a set of directions. It tells the search which candidate to keep.

Start with a quality construct: Atlas should diagnose the incident correctly. Translate it into observable criteria: identify the cause, connect it to evidence, and propose a remedy that addresses it. Then decide how those criteria combine. Each step adds assumptions.

CheckEval investigates decomposed binary questions as an alternative to broad ratings and reports improved agreement across evaluators in its experiments. HealthBench uses conversation-specific criteria authored by physicians. PaperBench uses a hierarchy of gradable subtasks to evaluate research replication. These designs make different qualities inspectable; none makes aggregation a neutral choice. [3] [4] [5]

Equal weighting has a hidden author: the person who decides how many questions to ask. Write six style checks and one correctness check, and style controls six-sevenths of the average. Dividing one criterion into three can change the score even if the underlying requirement stays the same.

Weighted score = correctness × (1 − citation weight) + citation presence × citation weight. This toy example assumes both rates come from the same task set; it is not a benchmark result. With JavaScript disabled, the 50% example remains visible.

At a low citation weight, A wins. At a higher weight, B wins. Neither assistant changes. The ranking expresses a choice about value.

Some requirements should be reported as conditions of success. A diagnosis dimension may require both the correct cause and supporting evidence; a release policy may block unauthorized actions regardless of average score. Such gates need especially reliable criteria, because one false failure can dominate the decision.

The denominator is another policy choice. If an assistant completes 60 of 100 assigned tasks and succeeds on 54, it succeeds on 90% of completed tasks but only 54% of assigned tasks. Here the other 40 are explicitly unfinished. If their grades are merely missing, call them unknown and separate the grading outage from agent failure.

Finally, standards evolve. EvalGen documents how seeing outputs changes the criteria people want to apply. Version the standard, and rerun the baseline when it changes. Otherwise you can confuse a new definition of success with a better system. [6]

THE POWERFUL IDEA

A score is a compressed theory of value. Keep enough of the original evidence to challenge it.
