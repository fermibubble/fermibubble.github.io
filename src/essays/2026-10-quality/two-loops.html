Production is rich in surprises and poor in answer keys. A user abandons a session. Was the answer bad, the task complete, or the user interrupted? Someone accepts a recommendation. Was it correct, or merely convincing?

A benchmark has the opposite bargain. It can provide a known target and repeatable conditions, but it represents only the situations its authors included. A test suite can become very precise about the wrong slice of reality.

A useful design connects two loops. The discovery loop watches real behavior and finds candidate problems. The comparison loop reconstructs selected problems well enough to evaluate changes. This is the architecture proposed here; real systems may use additional loops and online experiments.

The bridge from production to evaluation is a demanding piece of engineering. Capture the relevant request, redact unnecessary private details, preserve the evidence available at the time, and define success independently of the answer the agent happened to give. Then check whether the case actually separates competent behavior from plausible failure.

Clio demonstrates one part of the discovery problem: using models to surface aggregate patterns in large collections of real conversations while applying privacy protections. Its clusters can show what people are doing and where to investigate. A usage cluster does not, by itself, establish a product failure or its cause. [2]

The reverse bridge requires evidence too. A candidate that works on reconstructed cases can be evaluated in a suitable live experiment. Shadow execution helps when actions can be observed without changing the world; randomized deployment can estimate effects on measured outcomes when its design and traffic support that conclusion.

For Atlas, “include certificate status in the investigation” first has to work on controlled incidents. In production, the team then checks whether unsupported restart recommendations decline, whether investigations take longer, and whether new mistakes appear.

Personal memory introduces a useful boundary. A convention learned for one customer may help that customer and harm another. Keep such knowledge scoped, versioned, and reversible. Turning a local lesson into a global rule is a new hypothesis requiring broader evidence.

THE POWERFUL IDEA

The quality of the bridges determines whether experience becomes evidence.
