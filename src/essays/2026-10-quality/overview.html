An agent improves its score. A team ships the change. The same failure returns a week later.

Improvement becomes durable when the team can explain what changed, test whether it helped, and preserve the evidence for the next problem. The quality flywheel connects those steps. Hill climbing supplies a way to search; the surrounding loop determines whether the climb leads somewhere useful.

Preface

Atlas is a fictional on-call assistant for a shipping-label service. It reads alerts, inspects operational data, and proposes mitigations for an engineer to approve. Its story begins with an apparent success: adding workers clears a growing queue. The trouble is that Atlas has drawn the wrong conclusion about why it worked.

We will follow that incident from the first score through investigation, experimentation, and release—and ask what the next failure should inherit.
