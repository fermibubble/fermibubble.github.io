The tests pass. A change goes live. Websites stop responding.

A quality flywheel begins with the gap between those three sentences. What did the tests miss? Which change would prevent a repeat? How do we know the proposed fix will help somewhere beyond the original failure?

Preface

Our starting point is Cloudflare's outage on 2 July 2019. A new security rule consumed so much processing time that websites returned errors. The rule had passed its tests; those tests did not cover the way it could exhaust CPU.

The outage and Cloudflare's account are real. Atlas, the on-call AI assistant we use to explore the lessons, is hypothetical. Its examples ask a practical question: how would we teach an assistant to investigate a slow or unavailable website, then verify that it had learned something useful?

The chapters follow that question through measurement, investigation, hill climbing, release, and the knowledge left behind.
