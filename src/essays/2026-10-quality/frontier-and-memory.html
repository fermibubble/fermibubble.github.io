Return to Atlas. The first intervention made its answers more polished. A better investigation discovered that certificate evidence was being skipped. The team tested a way to surface that evidence, checked unrelated incidents, and evaluated the result in live use.

What remains after the fix? A reproducible incident. A grounded criterion. A judge tested against convincing wrong answers. A better investigation step. A comparison against the previous version. A detector for recurrence. That collection changes the starting point for the next problem.

Current research is automating pieces of this chain. It is useful to distinguish established building blocks from promising recent proposals. The following is a selected map of methods and open questions, checked on 4 October 2026. It is not a claim that one end-to-end architecture has been proven best.

The older Reflexion work helps explain the lineage: feedback becomes a verbal memory that can guide a later trial without changing model weights. The newer methods expand what can be stored, revised, and searched. A reflection becomes useful knowledge only after the evidence supports it. [23]

Three unresolved questions deserve attention. Can a system choose the next experiment for the information it will reveal, rather than the score it might win? Can it tell when its evaluator has become stale? Can it retain transferable lessons while removing shortcuts and obsolete assumptions? The methods above address parts of these questions; the whole problem remains open.

For a team building today, the practical starting point is one failure carried all the way through. Reproduce it. Identify the competing explanations. Test a targeted change. Confirm it on work the optimizer did not study. Observe what happens after release. Save the objects that made the conclusion trustworthy.

A useful measure of maturity is how much of the next investigation can reuse verified work. Less time reconstructing the incident. Less time debating an ambiguous score. More time testing the intervention that might change the outcome.

The flywheel gains momentum when the next failure meets a system that remembers how to learn.

  1. Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning
    Version checked: 2023-10-10

    Verbal reflection stored as episodic memory. NeurIPS 2023; depends on informative feedback and further attempts, rather than unsupported self-critique alone.

  2. Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models
    Version checked: 2026-03-29

    Evolving, curated context playbooks. ICLR 2026; v3. Lessons need validation, and the paper’s leaderboard comparison is historical.

  3. TextGrad: Automatic "Differentiation" via Text
    First posted: 2024-06-11

    Natural-language feedback through computational graphs. Textual gradients are critiques, not exact derivatives or guarantees of improvement.

  4. GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning
    Version checked: 2026-02-14

    Trace reflection and preservation of complementary candidates. ICLR 2026 Oral; this article uses v2, with six tasks, rather than the older four-task comparison.

  5. Judging LLM-as-a-Judge: Concerning Rubric Artifacts in LLM-based Automated Text Generation Evaluation
    First posted: 2026-08-31

    Counterfactual and rubric-only probes of judges. Accepted for EMNLP 2026; findings concern the evaluated benchmarks and do not establish a universal causal mechanism.

  6. AgentRx: Diagnosing AI Agent Failures from Execution Trajectories
    Version checked: 2026-08-31

    Constraints and evidence logs for trajectory diagnosis. Findings of EMNLP 2026; evaluated on a selected corpus of failures.

  7. DeFA: Dependency-Guided Failure Attribution for LLM Agents
    First posted: 2026-10-01

    Dependencies and failure propagation. 1 October 2026 preprint; downstream improvement is demonstrated in spreadsheet skill evolution. Broader transfer remains an open question.

  8. Scoring Higher, Answering Worse: Mitigating Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based RL via Protocol-Level Rubrics
    First posted: 2026-09-30

    Additive rewards and critical omissions. September 2026 preprint; the cited aggregation mitigates the measured degradation but does not restore the untrained baseline.
